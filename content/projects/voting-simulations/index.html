Simulate how the United States elects presidents and members of Congress

Use this interactive workspace to experiment with how the Electoral College, the Senate, and the House of Representatives translate votes into seats. Explore why 270 electoral votes wins the presidency, how statewide Senate races differ from district-based House races, and how state rules shape the voter experience.

Step 1 · Voters People cast ballots under state-specific rules for registration, ID, and ballot access.
Step 2 · States States certify winners. For president they send a slate of pledged electors; for Congress they certify each winning candidate directly.
Step 3 · National tally The Electoral College meets to choose the president (270 of 538 needed). Congress counts electoral votes and seats its members.

Presidential: Electoral College

State control of election rules

Because states run elections, the experience of voting can be very different. Registration deadlines, ID requirements, mail ballot access, and the availability of early in-person voting all vary by state.

Registration

Some states offer automatic or same-day registration. Others close registration weeks beforehand, limiting last-minute participation.

Voter ID

Strict photo ID states require government-issued identification at the polls. Others accept non-photo documents or use signature matching.

Mail ballots

A handful of states mail ballots to all voters, while others require an approved excuse to vote absentee.

Early voting

Early in-person voting can range from nearly a month to zero days, affecting how easily people can cast a ballot.
